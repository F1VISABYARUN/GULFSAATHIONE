# Gulf (GCC) Labour-Law Facts for Migrant Workers (status as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-21. Labels used below: **[OFFICIAL]** = government text or portal; **[SECONDARY]** = law firm or news report; **[RIGHTS]** = ILO/HRW/Amnesty/Migrant-Rights assessment; **[UNVERIFIED]** = only one secondary source, or a conflict that I could not resolve. Never state a fine amount that is not listed here with a source.

## 1. Passport confiscation by employers (UAE, KSA, Oman, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain)

### Takeaway
An employer keeping a worker's passport without consent is prohibited in UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar and Kuwait. Saudi Arabia has the clearest official fines: SAR 1,000–3,000 per worker for the private/support-labour sector, and SAR 2,000 per worker for domestic workers. Qatar's fine is up to QAR 25,000. Bahrain's law does **not explicitly** ban it (US State Dept TIP 2025). The voice agent can say "it is illegal for your employer to keep your passport against your will" for UAE/KSA/Oman/Qatar/Kuwait, but for Bahrain it should only say "it is a widely criticised practice; complain to LMRA".

### Cited Findings
**UAE**
- Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 (in force 2 Feb 2022, replaced Federal Law 8 of 1980), Article 13: an employer may not keep a worker's official documents (passport, Emirates ID), including as security for debts or to stop the worker leaving. [SECONDARY] — [Legal500 / Dr Hassan Elhais](https://www.legal500.com/developments/press-releases/can-an-employer-keep-your-passport-in-the-uae-heres-what-the-law-says/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/ask-gulf-news/ask-gulf-news-can-an-employer-keep-your-passport-in-the-uae-heres-what-the-law-says-1.500568227). Official text: [MOHRE PDF of Decree-Law 33/2021 with amendments](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/assets/download/3bde4984/Federal%20Decree-Law%20No.%2033%20of%202021%20Regarding%20the%20Regulation%20of%20Employment%20Relationship%20and%20its%20amendments_638990571068264034.pdf.aspx) (I did not read the article text directly).
- Penalty: general fine of AED 5,000–1,000,000 for violating the Decree-Law, multiplied by the number of affected workers up to AED 10 million. **[UNVERIFIED]** The article numbers (62/63) come from one secondary source, and Gulf News cites different article numbering. — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/press-releases/can-an-employer-keep-your-passport-in-the-uae-heres-what-the-law-says/); [Gulf News (Dh10m fines)](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/new-uae-labour-law-up-to-dh10-million-fines-for-violating-employees-rights-1.1643632120921)

**Saudi Arabia**
- HRSD "Violations and Penalties Applicable to the Support Labor Sector (Recruitment)" (PDF posted Aug 2026), General Violations row 31, "The employer's retention of a worker's passport or residence permit (iqama)": classified as Severe, **SAR 1,000 (under 20 workers) / 2,000 (21–49) / 3,000 (50+), applied per worker**. [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf)
- The same HRSD table, Domestic Workers section, row 12: a domestic employer keeping the worker's passport, personal documents, ID or belongings is fined **SAR 2,000 per worker**. Row 6: blocking contact with family or the embassy is SAR 1,000 per worker. [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf)
- The same table, Agricultural workers/private shepherds section, row 10: keeping a passport is **SAR 3,000 per worker**. [OFFICIAL] — same PDF
- 2026 news reports give a flat SAR 3,000 per worker. Earlier schedules were SAR 2,000 (pre-2019) and SAR 5,000 (2019), and a 2025 draft proposed SAR 1,000. Use the HRSD table, not the older figures. — [Gulf News 2026](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-revises-labour-penalties-sets-sr10000-fine-for-hiring-foreign-workers-without-permits-1.500455755); [Saudi Gazette proposal](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/652019/SAUDI-ARABIA/HR-Ministry-proposes-revised-penalties-for-Labor-Law-violations)
- Related rows in the same HRSD table: forced labour SAR 1,000–3,000 per worker (row 35); employer passing its fees on to workers SAR 1,000–3,000 per worker (row 32); failure to return documents and give a service certificate at the end of the job SAR 1,000–3,000 per worker (row 37). [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf)

**Oman**
- Labour Law issued by Royal Decree 53/2023 (published July 2023): the employer may not keep a worker's passport or personal documents without the worker's written consent. This is the first explicit ban in Omani labour law. [OFFICIAL text] — [decree.om RD 53/2023](https://decree.om/2023/rd20230053/); [Migrant-Rights.org overview](https://www.migrant-rights.org/2023/08/omans-new-labour-law-an-overview/)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Article number: decree.om places the rule in Article 7, while a Times of Oman interview says Article 6. — [Times of Oman](https://timesofoman.com/article/160907-oman-guarantees-expatriate-workers-right-to-retain-passports)
- Penalty under RD 53/2023: **not found**. The figure of OMR 100 and/or one month in jail is from the repealed 2003 law. Do not use it.

**Qatar**
- Law No. 21 of 2015 (entry, exit and residence of expatriates), Article 8: the employer must hand the passport back to the worker once residence procedures are complete. The employer may hold it only at the worker's written request. — [GLMM summary](https://gulfmigration.grc.net/law-no-21-of-2015-regulating-the-entry-exit-and-residence-of-expatriates/?print=pdf); [Squire Patton Boggs](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/-/media/files/insights/publications/2016/04/new-sponsorship-law/22837--new-sponsorship-law--alert-v3.pdf)
- Fine: **up to QAR 25,000**. The Government of Qatar describes it as "QR25,000 for each instance". — [The Peninsula (citing Ministry)](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/20/12/2021/3-year-imprisonment-for-illegal-visa-trading-qr25000-fine-for-withholding-passport); [GCO response to Amnesty](https://www.gco.gov.qa/en/government-of-the-state-of-qatars-response-to-amnesty-international-report-new-name-old-system/)

**Kuwait**
- Withholding passports is prohibited. Arab Times cites Ministerial Resolution 143/A/2010, Article 1 (under Private Sector Labour Law 6/2010). — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/it-is-illegal-to-keep-employees-passports-in-kuwait); [US TIP 2019 Kuwait](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2010842.html) ("prohibited ... but this practice remained commonplace")
- Fine amount: **not found**. The new Residency Law (Decree-Law 114/2024, in force 5 Jan 2025) was not confirmed to contain a passport-retention penalty. — [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/21743/kuwait/other-news/amir-approves-new-residency-law)

**Bahrain**
- The US State Dept 2025 TIP report says Bahraini law "did not explicitly prohibit passport retention" and that confiscation remained widespread. Older HRW (2009) and Migrant-Rights (2013) pieces called it illegal. **This conflict is unresolved.** — [US TIP 2025 Bahrain via ecoi.net](https://www.ecoi.net/de/dokument/2130519.html); [HRW 2009](https://www.hrw.org/news/2009/11/04/bahrain-migrant-workers-denied-pay-right-travel)

### Inferences
- For the risk engine: "employer holds my passport" should raise risk in all six countries. Fine amounts should be quoted only for KSA (from the HRSD table) and Qatar (up to QAR 25,000).
- The worker's consent language matters. Oman and Qatar allow the employer to hold the passport with the worker's *written* request or consent, so the agent should say "without your consent".

- Exact UAE penalty article numbers.
- Oman penalty under RD 53/2023.
- Kuwait fine amount.
- The Bahrain statutory position.

## 2. Wage protection systems and how workers complain

### Takeaway
All six states run an electronic Wage Protection System (WPS) in which salaries are paid through banks and monitored by the labour ministry. The WPS only *monitors* payment. It does not guarantee pay if the employer goes bust, except through funds such as Qatar's WSIF (Workers' Support and Insurance Fund). Complaint channels: UAE MOHRE (600590000, app, WhatsApp); Saudi HRSD amicable settlement, then labour court (Qiwa for private sector, Musaned for domestic workers); Qatar MoL unified complaints platform; Oman MoL; Bahrain LMRA; Kuwait PAM.

### Cited Findings
**UAE**
- Ministerial Resolution No. 340 of 2026 replaces Resolution 598/2022 and is **in force 1 June 2026**. The month's salary is due on the **1st of the following month**. The compliance threshold rose from 80% to 85% of wages due. Escalation for late payment: day 2 warnings; day 5 suspension of new work permits; day 11 fines and downgrade to category 3 for repeat breaches; day 16 automatic registration of a labour dispute in some cases; day 21 referral to the Public Prosecution, asset attachment and travel bans for larger or repeat offenders. [SECONDARY] — [Clyde & Co, June 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/06/uae-introduces-stricter-wage-protection); [EY alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/uae-introduces-enhanced-wage-protection-system-effective-1-june-2026)
- The WPS covers all MOHRE-registered establishments. Complaints go through the MOHRE app/website or **600590000** (phone and WhatsApp). Confidential wage complaints are possible. — [u.ae Payment of wages](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/payment-of-wages); [Gulf News on anonymous reporting](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/how-to-report-unpaid-salaries-anonymously-in-the-uae-1.500214062)
- **No general minimum wage** in UAE law, only a requirement that wages meet basic needs. — [u.ae Payment of wages](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/payment-of-wages)

**Saudi Arabia**
- HRSD penalty table: failing to pay wages on the due date into an approved bank account, or withholding wages without a judicial basis, is fined SAR 300 per worker (row 39). Failing to submit the monthly WPS file or meet the compliance rate is SAR 500/1,000/2,000 by establishment size (row 40). Failing to pay end-of-service dues within 1 week of termination (or 2 weeks if the worker resigned) is SAR 1,000–3,000 per worker (row 36). Domestic workers: non-payment of wages SAR 3,000 per worker; not documenting wages per the compliance plan SAR 1,000. [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf)
- Contract disputes are not handled in Qiwa. They go to HRSD's **amicable settlement** service, then the labour court. — [KPMG Flash Alert 2026-256 (7 Oct 2026)](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-256.html)
- Since Oct 2025, domestic employers may not charge domestic workers any fees (recruitment, permit, transfer, profession change). Reported fines are up to SAR 20,000 plus a 3-year recruitment ban. [SECONDARY] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/gulf/saudi-changes-law-employing-domestic-worker)
- Saudi Arabia has **no minimum wage for migrant workers**: I found no source for one, and none should be stated.

**Qatar**
- The WPS monitors payment of all private-sector workers and was "enhanced following independent assessment". The online complaints platform received 34,425 complaints between Oct 2021 and Oct 2022. The Workers' Support and Insurance Fund (Law 17/2018, operational 2019) has paid out more than USD 350m. [RIGHTS/ILO] — [ILO overview of Qatar reforms](https://www.ilo.org/resource/overview-qatars-labour-reforms)
- HRW (Dec 2025): the WPS and WSIF are "insufficient to prevent and remedy rampant wage theft". The WSIF pays only after a court/committee ruling that the employer cannot pay. [RIGHTS] — [HRW Dec 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/12/14/qatar-failure-to-pay-contractors-harms-migrant-workers); [HRW 2020](https://www.hrw.org/news/2020/12/22/qatar-wage-abuse-action-shortchanges-workers)

**Oman**
- WPS made mandatory under RD 53/2023 from July 2023 and phased in. Ministerial Resolution 729/2024 (Gazette 15 Dec 2024) set the phases. Wages must be transferred within **3 days** of the due date (previously 7). Coverage requirement: 75% of the workforce from September 2025 wages. [SECONDARY] — [KPMG Oman Jan 2025](https://kpmg.com/om/en/insights/2025/01/ministry-of-labour-updates-to-the-wage-protection-system-in-oman.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/oman-sets-new-wage-transfer-compliance-deadlines-for-companies-1.500230698); [MoL WPS PDF](https://www.mol.gov.om/CMSUploadFolder/Site/SliderFiles/WPS-EN.pdf)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** A 90% requirement from November 2025 is reported only by a payroll vendor blog. — [greytHR](https://www.greythr.com/middle-east/blog/oman-wps-update-compliance-guide-2026/)

**Bahrain**
- WPS has been legally required since LMRA Resolution No. 68 of 2019. From **January 2026**, LMRA mandates "WPS 2.0" for all private-sector employers through its central portal. [SECONDARY] — [PwC GCC social security alert Q1 2026](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/2026/social-security-alert-gcc-q1-2026.pdf)
- Bahrain Penal Code Art. 302 criminalises wage withholding (per HRW 2009). — [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2009/11/04/bahrain-migrant-workers-denied-pay-right-travel)

**Kuwait**
- I found no reliable 2025–2026 source on Kuwait's WPS or the PAM complaint procedure in this research.

**GCC-wide**
- Qatar MoL presented at the ILO in Nov 2025, and a GCC-wide WPS study was launched. — [Qatar MOFA, 25 Nov 2025](https://mofa.gov.qa/en/qatar/latest-articles/latest-news/details/2025/11/25/study-on-gcc-wage-protection-system-launched)

### Inferences
- In the UAE, a salary unpaid after the 1st of the next month is now formally "late". A worker unpaid for more than 15 days could reasonably be told that MOHRE may already be auto-registering a dispute, but they should still file a complaint themselves.
- "Salary paid in cash / not into a bank" is a red flag in KSA, Oman, UAE and Qatar, because all four require bank/WPS payment.

### Gaps
- Kuwait WPS and PAM complaint steps.
- Oman MoL complaint channel name and number.
- Bahrain LMRA complaint hotline.
- The official text of UAE Resolution 340/2026, which I read only through a law-firm summary.

## 3. Saudi Arabia: huroob / "absent from work" (taghayyub an al-amal), the 2021 Labour Reform Initiative, 2022–2026 changes, domestic workers

### Takeaway
"Huroob" (absconding) is the colloquial name for the employer filing an "absent from work" report. Officially it is now called "absent from work" or a "work-interruption report" (*inqita' an al-amal*). Since 2025 (Qiwa, private sector), a worker flagged absent gets a **60-day window** to transfer to a new employer, leave the country, or re-contract. From Sept/Oct 2026, a worker with **more than 12 months in KSA** can transfer through Qiwa despite "absent" status, with the new employer paying outstanding permit fees. The 2021 Labour Reform Initiative (LRI) gave private-sector workers exit/re-entry, final exit and conditional job change without employer permission, but **domestic workers were excluded**. Domestic workers follow a separate Musaned-based process.

### Cited Findings
- **LRI** took effect **14 March 2021** and applies to private-sector expatriates. Main elements:
  - Job mobility without employer consent after completing 1 year of the contract, or when the contract expires.
  - Exit/re-entry visa without employer permission (single use, valid 30 days).
  - Final exit without employer permission.

  [SECONDARY] — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/additional-details-released-on-labor-reform-initiative-for-the-private-sector.html); [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/reforms-to-saudis-sponsorship-kafala-system-and-rights-groups-say-these-reforms-are-not-adequate); [HRSD LRI page](https://hrsd.gov.sa/en/node/770957)
- The LRI **excludes** workers not covered by the Labour Law: domestic workers, farmers, shepherds, home guards and private drivers (sources list these slightly differently). — [HRW 2021](https://www.hrw.org/news/2021/03/25/saudi-arabia-labor-reforms-insufficient); [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2021/6/21/duterte-urges-saudi-arabia-to-include-domestic-workers-in-labor-reform-initiative)
- HRW's critique: the reforms "by no means dismantle the kafala system". Workers still need employer permission to change jobs if they have less than 1 year or an unfinished contract. Employers still control residency and can still file absconding reports. [RIGHTS] — [HRW 2021](https://www.hrw.org/news/2021/03/25/saudi-arabia-labor-reforms-insufficient)
- **Absence report, 2025 Qiwa update**: an employer may file an absence report only if the worker's iqama has at least 60 days of validity and there is no active contract. After the status change, the worker has **60 days** to transfer, leave the Kingdom, or re-sign with the same employer. Reported mid-2025. [SECONDARY] — [DLA Piper 2025](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/saudi-arabia-grants-expat-workers-60-days-to-avoid-being-classified-as-absent); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-grants-expat-workers-60-days-to-avoid-being-classified-as-absconders-1.500217793); [Saudi Gazette](https://www.saudigazette.com.sa/article/653810/SAUDI-ARABIA/Qiwa-updates-employment-rules-on-worker-absence-and-contract-termination)
- **Qiwa clarification (Saudi Gazette 30 Sep 2026; KPMG 7 Oct 2026)**:
  - A worker whose status changed to "absent" (after an absence report, a work-interruption report or contract termination) may transfer via Qiwa if they have spent **more than 12 months** in KSA.
  - The new employer pays outstanding work-permit fees.
  - The establishment may remove a worker shown in Absher/Muqeem as "left and did not return" or "final exit".

  [SECONDARY] — [KPMG Flash Alert 2026-256](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-256.html); [Envoy Global](https://www.envoyglobal.com/news-alert/the-kingdom-of-saudi-arabia-expands-employer-transfer-options-for-foreign-workers-with-absent-status/)
- The HRSD official document uses "absent from work" as the status under which a new employer can apply via Qiwa without the previous employer's consent. [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF (Oct 2022)](https://prod.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2022-10/E23102022.pdf)
- **Domestic workers (Musaned)**, from April 2024:
  - If the contract is ended for absence within 2 years of entry, the worker must take final exit within 60 days.
  - After 2 years, the worker can either leave or transfer within 60 days.
  - "Contract termination due to absence from work" and "labor mobility" are Musaned services.

  — [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/641833); [HRSD news Apr 2024](https://hrsd.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/040420241)
- Domestic-worker amnesty: a 6-month window from 11 May 2025 let domestic workers reported absent transfer via Musaned. It was extended 6 months from 11 Nov 2025 and ended around 11 May 2026. **Any further extension is unverified.** — [Gulf News May 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-offers-six-month-amnesty-for-runaway-domestic-workers-to-legalise-status-1.500124094); [Gulf News Nov 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-extends-grace-period-for-runaway-domestic-workers-1.500342505); [Wego](https://blog.wego.com/saudi-domestic-worker-grace-period/)
- HRSD table, domestic-worker penalties:
  - Worker self-employed or working for another employer: SAR 2,000 (row 20).
  - Repeated unjustified absences, 3 times in a year: SAR 500 for the worker (row 22).
  - Employer assigning the domestic worker to third parties: SAR 5,000 per worker (row 9).

  [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf)
- Domestic workers' entitlements under the 2025 rules (reported): full pay, a weekly rest day, daily breaks, and one month of paid leave every 2 years. [SECONDARY] — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/gulf/saudi-changes-law-employing-domestic-worker)

### Inferences
- The traditional consequences of huroob are illegal status, risk of arrest or detention and deportation, and inability to transfer. These are softened but not removed. A worker flagged absent must act within 60 days (private sector) or the stated Musaned window (domestic workers). The voice agent should push the worker to check their status on Absher/Qiwa/Musaned immediately.
- There is no source showing the absence report has been "abolished". It has been renamed and given grace periods. Do not tell users huroob no longer exists.

### Gaps
- An official Qiwa/HRSD text for the 60-day rule and for the 2026 12-month rule. I have secondary reports only.
- The exact detention or entry-ban consequences after the 60 days lapse.
- Whether the domestic-worker amnesty was extended past May 2026.

## 4. Working on a visit/tourist visa

### Takeaway
Working on a visit visa is illegal in all six GCC states and leads to fines, detention, deportation and possible re-entry bans. Specific fine figures were found only for the UAE (worker: minimum AED 10,000 per secondary reports; employer: up to AED 1m) and Saudi Arabia (HRSD: misusing a domestic visa costs SAR 10,000 plus a 1-year recruitment ban for the employer). For Qatar, Oman, Kuwait and Bahrain, only general statements were found.

### Cited Findings
- **UAE**: using a visa for a purpose other than its intended one (e.g., working on a visit visa) carries a minimum fine of AED 10,000, possible imprisonment and deportation. Employers face fines of AED 100,000–1,000,000 for allowing illegal work. **[UNVERIFIED]**: secondary news, and the exact decree article was not confirmed. — [Gulf News: 7 UAE visa violations](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/visa-immigration/dh5-million-fine-7-uae-visa-violations-that-could-lead-to-jail-and-deportation-1.500376438); [Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-labour-law-changes-hefty-fines-to-prevent-firms-from-hiring-visit-visa-holders)
- UAE Decree-Law 33/2021 penalises employing a worker without a work permit (Gulf News cites up to AED 200,000 under Art. 60). Article numbering is inconsistent across sources. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/new-uae-labour-law-up-to-dh10-million-fines-for-violating-employees-rights-1.1643632120921)
- **Saudi Arabia**: HRSD table, domestic workers row 11, "Using the visa for purposes other than those for which it was issued": SAR 10,000 per worker plus a 1-year recruitment ban for the employer. [OFFICIAL] — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf). Overstaying an entry visa: up to 6 months in prison, up to SAR 50,000 fine and deportation. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-warns-of-jail-fine-and-deportation-for-visa-overstayers-1.500521208)
- **Oman**: working without a valid work visa can lead to fines, detention or deportation. Since 2021, a visit visa can be converted to a work visa inside the country (this legal route must go through a proper employer). [SECONDARY] — [Remofirst Oman](https://www.remofirst.com/visa-guide/oman); [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/en/oman-allows-expats-convert-visit-visas-work-permit/)
- **Bahrain**: paid or unpaid work on a visit visa is "absolutely illegal", and workers caught in crackdowns face deportation. [SECONDARY] — [News of Bahrain](https://newsofbahrain.com/bahrain/82398.html)
- **Kuwait**: the Residency Law (Decree-Law 114/2024, effective 5 Jan 2025) sets overstay fines. Figures conflict: KD 10/day up to KD 2,000 per one report, versus KD 2–4/day up to KD 1,200 per Deloitte. Domestic workers: KD 2/day up to KD 600. Residency trafficking: 3–5 years in prison and KD 5,000–10,000 (from draft-stage reporting). **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/expats-face-hefty-fines-under-kuwaits-new-residency-law); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/services/tax/perspectives/deloitte-immigration-news-kuwait-important-updates-on-penalties-and-residency-law-violations.html)
- **Qatar**: no specific current penalty found for working on a visit visa.

### Inferences
- The risk engine should treat "agent says go on a visit visa, the work visa will come later" as **high risk** in every GCC country. The worker carries the penalty (fine, deportation, ban), not only the employer.

### Gaps
- Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain official visit-visa work penalties.
- Whether the UAE "life ban" for visit-visa workers is still in force (the source is old and undated).

## 5. Qatar's 2020 reforms and minimum wage facts (Qatar, UAE, others)

### Takeaway
Qatar's changes:
- Exit permits were removed: for Labour Law workers in 2018, and for others, including domestic workers, by Interior Ministerial Decision 95/2019 (effective 16 Jan 2020).
- The NOC (employer's permission) to change jobs was removed by Law 18/2020 (and Law 19/2020), in effect from about Nov 2020.
- A non-discriminatory minimum wage of QAR 1,000 basic, plus QAR 500 housing and QAR 300 food where the employer does not provide them (total QAR 1,800), was set by Law 17/2020 and in force from March 2021. It covers all nationalities and sectors, including domestic workers.

These reforms remain in law as of 2026. No other GCC state has a general minimum wage for migrants.

### Cited Findings
- Law 17/2020: minimum wage QAR 1,000 a month, plus QAR 500 housing and QAR 300 food if not provided. It applies to all workers, all nationalities and all sectors, including domestic workers. — [ILO: Qatar adopts non-discriminatory minimum wage](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/qatar-adopts-non-discriminatory-minimum-wage); [GCO statement](https://www.gco.gov.qa/en/top-news/adlsa-statement-minimum-wage-noc/)
- In effect from **March 2021**. The total thresholds are QR 1,800 (about USD 500). About 280,000 workers had their basic wage raised. — [ILO overview](https://www.ilo.org/resource/overview-qatars-labour-reforms)
- Law 18/2020 amended Labour Law 14/2004 to remove the NOC requirement. More than 348,500 job changes were approved between 1 Nov 2020 and 31 Aug 2022. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/08/30/minimum-wage-no-noc-qatar-announces-changes-to-labour-law/); [ILO overview](https://www.ilo.org/resource/overview-qatars-labour-reforms)
- Exit permits: Interior Ministerial Decision No. 95 of 2019, effective 16 Jan 2020, removed exit permits for expatriates *not* covered by the Labour Law, including domestic workers. Labour Law workers had already been exempted in 2018. — [GCO labour reform page](https://www.gco.gov.qa/en/focus/labour-reform/)
- Remaining problems per the ILO: workers face retaliation and misinformation when changing jobs, months-long wage delays, slow court enforcement, and domestic workers' working time and days off. [RIGHTS/ILO] — [ILO overview](https://www.ilo.org/resource/overview-qatars-labour-reforms)
- Amnesty (2020) welcomed the reforms but said the minimum wage "remains low". — [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2020/08/qatar-annoucement-kafala-reforms-2/)
- UAE: **no minimum wage** in the labour law. — [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/payment-of-wages)

### Inferences
- An offer in Qatar paying less than QAR 1,000 basic, or less than QAR 1,800 with no food or housing provided, is unlawful. This is a deterministic rule the risk engine can use.
- India-specific "minimum referral wages" for emigration clearance (eMigrate) may matter for UAE/KSA/Kuwait/Oman/Bahrain offers. They are out of scope here and not verified.

### Gaps
- Whether Qatar has revised the QAR 1,000 figure since 2021. The Minimum Wage Commission reviews it, and I found no change.
- Minimum wages for nationals (e.g., Saudization or Emiratisation salary floors) were not researched, because they do not apply to migrants.

## 6. Kafala: which elements remain, by country (government claims vs rights-group assessments)

### Takeaway
Governments in Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Oman describe kafala as reformed or abolished. Rights groups (HRW, Amnesty, Migrant-Rights, UN experts) say its core elements remain everywhere: employer-tied residency/visa, employer-filed absconding reports, and domestic-worker exclusions. Kuwait **moved backwards in July 2025** by bringing back employer-approved exit permits for private-sector expatriates.

### Cited Findings
- **Saudi Arabia**:
  - Government/aligned framing: Walk Free (Oct 2025) reported that Saudi Arabia "has moved to abolish the kafala system". — [Walk Free 24 Oct 2025](https://www.walkfree.org/?p=35399)
  - Rights view: Amnesty (June 2025 joint letter) urged Riyadh to "fully abolish" kafala. — [Amnesty PDF](https://www.amnesty.org/zh-hans/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/MDE2394962025ENGLISH.pdf)
  - UN experts (29 Apr 2026, via HRW) urged KSA to "effectively abolish" kafala, so the system was still in place in 2026. — [HRW, 19 May 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/05/19/un-experts-sound-alarm-over-saudi-arabias-abusive-labor-governance-system)
  - The ILO and KSA launched phase 3 of technical cooperation in June 2025 (labour mobility, wage protection; USD 4.2m). — [ILO GCC factsheet Dec 2025](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/GCC-%20factsheet.pdf)
- **Kuwait**:
  - **Ministerial Circular No. 2 of 2025, effective 1 July 2025**: private-sector expatriates need an employer-approved exit permit, requested via the Sahel app and valid for 7 days, for any departure, temporary or permanent. — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/new-exit-permit-rule-effective-1-july-2025); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/middle-east/en/services/tax/perspectives/kuwait-introduction-of-an-exit-permit-for-expatriate-workers-in-kuwait.html)
  - HRW called this "an alarming step backward" that violates the right to leave any country. [RIGHTS] — [HRW, 15 June 2025](https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/06/15/kuwaits-exit-permit-requirement-puts-migrant-workers-at-risk)
  - Also in force: the new Residency Law, Decree-Law 114/2024 (effective 5 Jan 2025). — [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/21743/kuwait/other-news/amir-approves-new-residency-law)
- **Qatar**: exit permits and NOC removed (see section 5). The ILO says workers still face retaliation when changing jobs. — [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/overview-qatars-labour-reforms)
- **Oman**: the NOC requirement was removed from January 2021. — [Migration Policy Institute](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/article/gulf-region-gcc-migration-kafala-reforms). RD 53/2023 added the passport ban and repatriation duties. Migrant-Rights says more is needed to abolish kafala. — [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/oman-new-labour-laws-bring-some-protections-for-migrant-workers-but-advocates-says-more-must-be-done-to-abolish-the-kafala-system)
- **Bahrain**: the Flexi-Permit (2017) was cancelled in Oct 2022 and replaced by the LMRA "Labour Registration Program" (from 4 Dec 2022), run through accredited private registration centres. Rights groups pair it with stepped-up arrests and deportations. — [Migrant-Rights.org Oct 2022](https://www.migrant-rights.org/2022/10/bahrain-ends-its-flexi-permit-program-and-announces-labour-reforms/); [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/bahrain-privately-run-labour-registration-program-replaces-flexi-permit-scheme-as-govt-steps-up-arrests-deportations/)
- **UAE**: private-sector labour law rewritten (Decree-Law 33/2021). Per the Wilson Center (2021), workers in every GCC state except KSA could leave without employer permission. That changed for Kuwait in July 2025. — [Wilson Center](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/node/110744)
- Academic/rights framing: "Reforms without Rights" (GLMM, 2024). — [GLMM paper](https://gulfmigration.grc.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Kalush-Saraswathi-Reforms-without-Rights-Published-2024-09-09.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the agent's script, the remaining elements by country:

  | Country | Remaining or reintroduced elements |
  |---|---|
  | Kuwait | Employer exit permit (since July 2025) |
  | Saudi Arabia | Job change limited in the first year; absence/huroob reports; domestic workers outside the LRI |
  | All six | Employer-sponsored residency; domestic workers with weaker protection |

- No current Migrant-Rights.org country-by-country 2025–2026 scorecard was retrieved.
- UAE-specific rights-group assessments for 2025–2026 were not retrieved.

## 7. Glossary verification

### Takeaway
Most terms are confirmed in context by the sources above. "Arbab" and the formal "Emirates ID" issuer were not confirmed by a fetched source in this round.

### Cited Findings
- **Kafeel / kafala**: sponsor / the sponsorship system tying a migrant's residency to the employer. — [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2020/10/30/what-will-it-take-saudi-arabia-abolish-abusive-sponsorship-system); [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/dismantling-kafala-system-and-introducing-minimum-wage-mark-new-era-qatar)
- **Iqama**: Saudi residence permit. The HRSD table pairs "passport or residence permit (iqama)". — [HRSD PDF](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-08/almkhalfat-wal-qwbat-alkhast-bqta--al-malt-almsandt--alastqdam----en-.pdf)
- **Huroob**: colloquial "absconding". The official KSA term is "absent from work", or a work-interruption report. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-256.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-grants-expat-workers-60-days-to-avoid-being-classified-as-absconders-1.500217793)
- **Khurooj nihai / final exit**: a visa to leave KSA permanently. Since the March 2021 LRI it can be obtained without employer permission (private sector only). — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/additional-details-released-on-labor-reform-initiative-for-the-private-sector.html). Exit/re-entry is the separate return-trip visa.
- **Qiwa**: HRSD digital platform for private-sector labour services (contracts, transfers, absence status). — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-256.html); [HRSD](https://prod.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2022-10/E23102022.pdf)
- **Musaned**: HRSD platform for domestic workers (recruitment, contracts, absence and transfer). — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-offers-six-month-amnesty-for-runaway-domestic-workers-to-legalise-status-1.500124094); [HRSD](https://hrsd.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/040420241)
- **Absher / Muqeem**: Interior Ministry e-services (Absher for individuals, Muqeem for establishments), where residency status such as "left and did not return" or "final exit" appears. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-256.html)
- **MOHRE**: UAE Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (labour ministry; WPS; complaints on 600590000). — [MOHRE WPS page](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/en/projects/wage-protection.aspx); [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/sector-of-employment/employment-in-the-private-sector/payment-of-wages)
- **Emirates ID**: the UAE identity card, treated with the passport as an "official document" an employer may not withhold. — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/press-releases/can-an-employer-keep-your-passport-in-the-uae-heres-what-the-law-says/)
- **Labour card / work permit (UAE)**: issued by MOHRE. Employing someone without one is penalised under Decree-Law 33/2021. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/new-uae-labour-law-up-to-dh10-million-fines-for-violating-employees-rights-1.1643632120921)
- **Sahel**: Kuwait government app used for exit-permit requests (2025). — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/practice-area-articles/kuwait)
- **LMRA**: Bahrain Labour Market Regulatory Authority. **PAM**: Kuwait Public Authority for Manpower. — [Migrant-Rights.org](https://www.migrant-rights.org/2022/10/bahrain-ends-its-flexi-permit-program-and-announces-labour-reforms/); [People Matters](https://me.peoplemattersglobal.com/article/employment-landscape/no-expat-exit-without-permit-pam-kuwait-clarifies-regulations-46060)

### Inferences
- "Arbab" is widely used in South Asian / Malayalam and Telugu Gulf-migrant speech for "sponsor/boss" (from the Arabic plural of *rabb*, "owners/masters"). This is my own knowledge, with no fetched source. Mark it [UNVERIFIED] before the voice agent asserts it.
- The issuer of the Emirates ID is the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security (ICP). This is from my own knowledge and was not verified in this round.

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for "arbab".
- No primary source fetched for the issuer of the Emirates ID.
- "Labour card" is now largely replaced by the electronic work permit/contract in the UAE. The current MOHRE terminology was not verified.
