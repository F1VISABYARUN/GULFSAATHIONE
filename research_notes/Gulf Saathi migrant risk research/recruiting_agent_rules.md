# India: Recruiting Agent (RA) Rules for Overseas / Gulf (ECR) Workers — status as of Nov 2026

Research date: 2026-11-21. Primary texts were downloaded and read where possible (Gazette PDF, MEA PDFs, Lok Sabha answer PDF). mea.gov.in blocks some automated fetchers (HTTP 403) but returned pages to a browser user-agent. indiankanoon.org was unreachable from this session.

## 1. Maximum service charge an RA may collect

### Takeaway
The cap is **Rs 30,000 per worker, with GST (18%) charged on top**. It is set by Rule 25 of the Emigration Rules, 1983, as substituted by the Emigration (Amendment) Rules, 2017. MEA advisories, including Dec 2023 advisories still linked from MEA in 2026, state it as "Rs. 30,000 + GST (18%)". I found no later amendment to Rule 25. The 2023 amendment (G.S.R. 907(E)) changed only Rule 8, on security. Before 2017 the cap was "45 days' wages, maximum Rs 20,000", and an old MEA page still shows that figure.

### Cited Findings
- Verbatim text of substituted Rule 25(1): "No recruiting agent shall collect from the worker the service charges more than a maximum of thirty thousand rupees, in respect of services provided by it to that worker and the recruiting agent shall issue a receipt to the worker for the amount collected by it in this regard." — [Emigration (Amendment) Rules, 2017, text at LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Rule 25(2): what the Rs 30,000 **includes**:
  - (a) scouting and selecting candidates
  - (b) advertisements
  - (c) trade test fees, wherever applicable
  - (d) attestation charges for certificates
  - (e) domestic travel or lodging and boarding for interviews
  - (f) recurring annual cost of enhanced Bank Guarantee
  - (g) "cancellation charges imposed by foreign employers on recruiting agents when candidates refuse to travel and want early repatriation"
  - (h) administrative cost of head and branch offices

  An RA therefore cannot bill these items to the worker on top of the Rs 30,000. — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Rule 25(3): the service charge **excludes**:
  - (a) Embassy or Consular visa fees
  - (b) pre-employment medical check-up cost
  - (c) air ticket to the destination country
  - (d) boarding and lodging before deployment
  - (e) GST

  The proviso reads: "Provided that the charges under this sub-rule, except goods and services tax, shall be borne by the foreign employer." So visa, medical, air ticket and pre-deployment lodging are meant to be paid by the employer, not the worker. Only GST passes to the worker. — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Notification identity: the Gazette note to G.S.R. 907(E) lists the amendment history, including "21. G.S.R 1505(E), dated the 12th December, 2017; 22. G.S.R 1510(E), dated the 14th December, 2017". Search snippets of the Indian Kanoon copy tie the Rule 25 substitution to **G.S.R. 1510(E), 14 Dec 2017**. I could not open the Indian Kanoon page directly to confirm, so treat the mapping between 1505(E) and 1510(E) as unverified. — [Gazette G.S.R. 907(E), 20 Dec 2023 (thc.nic.in copy)](https://www.thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Rules/Emigration%20Rules,%201983..pdf)
- The 2023 amendment (G.S.R. 907(E), 20 Dec 2023) substituted only **Rule 8**, on the amount of security:
  - Rs 50 lakh bank guarantee or insurance with 8.5 years' validity; or
  - a reduced Rs 8 lakh security, which allows only 100 workers per registration period, plus Rs 8 lakh per additional tranche of 100.

  It did not touch Rule 25. — [Gazette G.S.R. 907(E)](https://www.thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Rules/Emigration%20Rules,%201983..pdf)
- MEA (OE & PGE Division) advisory, Dec 2023: "As per the Emigration Act 1983, no recruiting agent shall collect from the prospective emigrant the service charges more than Rs. 30,000 + GST (18%), in respect of services provided by it to that emigrant and the recruiting agent shall issue a receipt to the emigrant for the amount collected by it in this regard." The same advisory reports overcharging by unregistered agents "to the tune of Rs. 2-5 lakhs". — [MEA advisory via Embassy Vilnius, 19-12-2023](https://www.eoivilnius.gov.in/pdf/Advisory%20circular-19-12-23.pdf); also re-issued by [Govt of Goa (Aug 2024)](https://www.goa.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/ADVISORY-ON-OVERCHARGING.pdf)
- MEA's Overseas Employment page (fetched Nov 2026) still links "Advisory on overcharging by agents for overseas recruitment, offering fake overseas jobs and illegal recruitment" at images/pdf/05-List-2024.pdf. — [MEA Overseas Employment](https://www.mea.gov.in/overseas-employment.htm)
- **Outdated figure still online:** MEA's "RAs" page (fetched Nov 2026) says: "The charges which the recruiting agent may recover from an emigrant in respect of services rendered, shall not exceed. Equivalent to wages for 45 days as per the employment contract subject to maximum Rs.20,000/-". This is the pre-Dec-2017 rule and was superseded by the 2017 Rule 25. — [MEA ras.htm](https://www.mea.gov.in/ras.htm)

### Inferences
- Suggested app check: charged = service fee + 18% GST. Flag if the service fee is above Rs 30,000, which means the total is above Rs 35,400. Also flag any separate charge to the worker for visa, medical, air ticket or pre-departure lodging: these are excluded from the cap and the employer is meant to bear them. The PBBY insurance premium (Rs 275 or Rs 375) and PDOT are separately displayed charges, not part of the cap.
- GST is not in the rule text as "18%". The rule says "the goods and services tax as applicable", and the 18% comes from MEA advisories. If the GST rate on manpower services changes, the app should follow the rule's "as applicable" wording.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Gazette PDF of G.S.R. 1510(E) itself, so its exact publication date and number are confirmed only via search snippets and the amendment list.
- I found no evidence of any change to the Rs 30,000 cap between Dec 2017 and Nov 2026. This rests on the 2023 amendment list and the 2023–2026 advisories, not on an exhaustive Gazette search.

## 2. Payment, receipt and display rules

### Takeaway
Service charges may be paid **only by digital payment or bank draft. No cash.** The RA's undertaking specifies NEFT/RTGS or other government-approved digital modes. A receipt is mandatory. The RA must display a board showing the maximum service charge and the payment mode, plus the PBBY insurance and PDOT charges. It must also show a signboard with its name, registration number and year of registration.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 25(4)(a): "The service charges shall be collected by the recruiting agent only through digital payments or bank draft." — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Rule 25(4)(b) and Annexure-I: the notarized undertaking on Rs 100 stamp paper says:
  - "all payments to be collected under 'service charges' shall be collected only through NEFT or RTGS or any other digital transactions approved by the Government of India, and no payments shall be received in cash."
  - "No additional expenses on recruitment shall be defrayed on intending emigrant workers."
  - Non-adherence makes the RA "liable to penalties prescribed under section 24 and 25 of the Emigration Act, 1983."

  — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Rule 25(5): "Every recruiting agent shall also display in his or her premises a board indicating the maximum allowed service charge to be collected as well as the mode of payment, along with Pravasi Bharatiya Bima Yojana Insurance charges and Pre-Departure Orientation Training." — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Receipt: Rule 25(1) requires the RA to "issue a receipt to the worker for the amount collected". — [LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/emigration-amendment-rules-2017/945B)
- Terms of registration on the MEA RA page require "A signboard, to be displayed in front of the business premises or so fixed that it is conspicuously visible to the public from outside the office premises indicating the name and the registration number of the recruiting agent and the year of registration." — [MEA ras.htm](https://www.mea.gov.in/ras.htm)
- On license display, the MEA advisory says: "All registered RAs are issued a license number which is prominently displayed in their office premises and in their advertisements, including newspapers and social media." — [MEA advisory (Vilnius copy)](https://www.eoivilnius.gov.in/pdf/Advisory%20circular-19-12-23.pdf)
- PBBY is mandatory for RAs to purchase for workers:
  - Rs 10 lakh cover on death, plus work-related injury and medical expenses.
  - Premium Rs 275 for 2 years or Rs 375 for 3 years.

  — [MEA advisory](https://www.eoivilnius.gov.in/pdf/Advisory%20circular-19-12-23.pdf)
- A valid job offer comes with an Employment Contract signed by the foreign employer, the RA and the worker, and must allow emigration on an employment or work visa: "tourist visa should only be used for the purpose of tourism". — [MEA advisory](https://www.eoivilnius.gov.in/pdf/Advisory%20circular-19-12-23.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested app red flags:
  - cash demanded
  - payment to a personal UPI or account that does not match the RA's name
  - no receipt
  - no licence number in ads or WhatsApp messages
  - tourist or visit visa offered for a job
  - no contract signed by all three parties

  The rule says "digital payments", so UPI counts. Whether payment to a non-RA account is illegal is not stated in the rule; this is an inference.

### Gaps
- I found no rule text specifying the receipt's format or GST invoice contents.

## 3. Registration requirements, and how and where to verify an RA (eMigrate / MEA lists)

### Takeaway
No one may act as an RA without a Registration Certificate (RC) from the Protector General of Emigrants (Emigration Act s.10). RCs are issued only through eMigrate. There are two ways to verify:

- **The eMigrate portal (https://emigrate.gov.in)**: the "List of active RA" link. The portal is a JavaScript single-page app, so I could not machine-read the exact sub-URL.
- **MEA's downloadable PDF lists** on its Overseas Employment page:
  - "District and State wise list of Active RA" as on 08-04-2026: 10 pages, about 2,447 rows; fields are RA ID, name, signatory, state, district, RC number, address, email, phone, status and website.
  - A separate State-government RA list.

eMigrate also publishes a list of **unregistered/illegal agents**. Its count, per a Lok Sabha answer of 31 July 2026, is **3,505**.

### Cited Findings
- Lok Sabha Unstarred Q. 2190, answered 31-07-2026 (MoS Kirti Vardhan Singh):
  - "As per Section 10 of the Emigration Act, 1983, no person or agency can function as RA without a valid Registration Certificate (RC) issued by the Registering Authority, i.e., Protector General of Emigrants (PGE)."
  - "Issuance of RC is done through eMigrate Portal (https://emigrate.gov.in) only ... List of all the registered RAs in India is publicly available on the eMigrate portal. List of unregistered agents against whom complaints were received, has been published on the eMigrate portal to caution the prospective emigrants. As on date a total of 3,505 such unregistered agents have been notified on the eMigrate portal."

## 4. ECR vs ECNR passports, ECR countries (incl. GCC), emigration clearance

### Takeaway
Holders of an ECR-endorsed passport who go to an ECR country **for employment** must obtain emigration clearance from the Protector of Emigrants, via eMigrate. ECNR is the default: any passport not endorsed ECR. **All six GCC states** (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain) are ECR countries. MEA's own web page says "currently 17" ECR countries, while other MEA documents and media list 18, with South Sudan as the 18th.

### Cited Findings
- MEA page text:
  - "Emigration Act, 1983 provides that no citizen of India shall migrate unless he obtains emigration clearance from Protector of Emigrants ... certain countries (currently 17) ... have been categorized as Emigration Check Required (ECR) countries."
  - "Hence, all persons, having ECR endorsed passports and going to any of the 17 ECR countries for taking up employment require emigration clearance. However, ECR passport holders going to any ECR country for purposes other than employment do not require emigration clearance."
  - "No specific mention of ECNR is made on the passports and the ones not endorsed as ECR, automatically belong to ECNR category."
  - The ECR Suspension (ECRS) system was abolished w.e.f. 1 Oct 2007.

  — [MEA Emigration Abroad for Employment](https://www.mea.gov.in/emigration-abroad-for-emp.htm)
- The 18-country list per MEA-linked documents (as summarized by search): Afghanistan, Bahrain, Indonesia, Iraq, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Malaysia, Oman, Qatar, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Thailand, UAE, Yemen. — [MEA "Emigration Abroad for Employment" PDF](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/attach/9_Emigration_Abroad_for_Employment.pdf); [HCI Kuala Lumpur list of ECR countries (2021)](https://hcikl.gov.in/pdf/SPDC_list_of_ECR_countries_2021_sep5_8.pdf) (search-snippet level; neither PDF could be downloaded)
- The eMigrate portal is integrated with the Bureau of Immigration "to facilitate clearance of the emigrant workers holding ECR passports travelling to ECR countries for employment". — [Lok Sabha AU2190, 31-07-2026](https://www.sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2190_NjP8nJ.pdf)
- A 2020 requirement that ECNR workers going to the 18 countries register on eMigrate was announced and then withdrawn. I did not confirm whether it was later reinstated. — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/alerts/government-retracts-online-registration-rule-indian-nationals-traveling-18-countries)

### Inferences
- For a Gulf-bound worker with an ECR passport, a job abroad without emigration clearance through eMigrate is irregular. A tourist or visit visa used for work is a strong red flag.

### Gaps
- 17 vs 18 conflict: MEA's HTML page says 17 while other MEA documents say 18. South Sudan appears to be the difference, likely added after the page was written. I could not open an authoritative current notification.

## 5. Penalties for illegal recruitment; Emigration Bill status

### Takeaway
Emigration Act 1983, s.24(1) covers running an RA without a certificate (s.10), overcharging, and cheating an emigrant. The penalty is imprisonment **up to 2 years and a fine up to Rs 2,000**, with a minimum of 6 months and Rs 1,000 absent special reasons. Repeat offences carry double the penalty. In practice, complaints also go to police under the BNS 2023. The replacement law, the **Overseas Mobility (Facilitation and Welfare) Bill, 2025**, was released in draft in Oct 2025, with comments due 7 Nov 2025. As of the latest sources (mid-2026) it was still "proposed". I found no evidence it has been introduced in Parliament. The older Emigration Bill 2021 draft was not enacted.

### Cited Findings
- s.24(1): whoever "contravenes the provisions of section 10 or section 16 ... or collects from an emigrant any charges in excess of the limits prescribed under this Act, or cheats any emigrant, shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years and with fine which may extend to two thousand rupees. Provided that ... such imprisonment shall not be less than six months and such fine shall not be less than one thousand rupees."
  - Attempts carry the same punishment.
  - Contravening emigration-clearance conditions: up to 1 year and/or Rs 2,000.
  - Abetment: same punishment as the offence.
  - Repeat conviction: "double the penalty".

  The source is a bare-act site whose text has typos; verify against India Code. — [AdvocateKhoj, Emigration Act s.24](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/emigration/24.php)
- s.25: where a company commits the offence, every person in charge of it is also deemed guilty. — [AdvocateKhoj s.25](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/emigration/25.php)
- MEA refers complaints "to the State/UT police for investigation and prosecution under the relevant provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) and other legislations". — [Lok Sabha AU2190](https://www.sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2190_NjP8nJ.pdf)
- MEA advisory: illegal recruitment activities "are in violation of Emigration Act 1983 and amount to human trafficking, which is a punishable criminal offense". — [MEA advisory](https://www.eoivilnius.gov.in/pdf/Advisory%20circular-19-12-23.pdf)
- Overseas Mobility (Facilitation and Welfare) Bill, 2025: draft published by MEA for comments in Oct 2025; it would replace the Emigration Act 1983 and create an Overseas Mobility and Welfare Council. — [MEA bill page](https://www.mea.gov.in/overseasmobilitybill2025.htm); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/govt-makes-public-draft-bill-to-replace-emigration-act-1983-3759149); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/mea/mea-proposes-overseas-mobility-bill-to-streamline-emigration-boost-workers-welfare)
- In July 2026 the government released stakeholder recommendations on the "proposed" bill. — [The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/government-releases-stakeholder-recommendations-on-proposed-overseas-mobility-bill)

### Inferences
- The Emigration Act 1983 and its Rules, including Rule 25, remain the law in force as of Nov 2026.

### Gaps
- I found no Lok Sabha bill number or introduction date for the 2025 Bill. Its penalty amounts in the draft were not extracted.
