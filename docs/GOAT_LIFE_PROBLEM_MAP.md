# The Goat Life → problem map for Gulf Saathi

**Honesty note.** Claude can't watch films. This is built from plot summaries and reviews of *Aadujeevitham / The Goat Life* (2024, dir. Blessy, based on Benyamin's 2008 novel *Goat Days*), reporting on the real Najeeb Muhammad, and news and legal sources on Gulf migration.
- The film and the novel are **dramatised**. Benyamin himself has said "Aadujeevitham is not Shukur's [Najeeb's] life story, it is my novel."
- Reporting says the real Najeeb spent about 3.5 years (1992–1995) on a desert farm in Saudi Arabia.
- Use the film as an **emotional reference everyone knows**, not as evidence. Use the news and legal sources below as evidence.
- Don't use film clips or images in the demo (copyright). Mentioning it verbally in the pitch is fine.

## 1. Najeeb's journey, stage by stage

| # | Stage | What goes wrong (film/novel) | What *should* have existed | Why nobody helped | What Saathi does (harness role) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Village / recruitment** | Goes on a visa arranged through an agent, on trust. No real knowledge of the employer or job. | Licensed agent, written contract, fee receipt (legal cap ₹30k + GST **[verify current]**) | Only a verbal promise, no record. Family trusts the agent. | **Promise call recorded and structured:** agent, employer, job, salary, hours, fee. Agent checked against the licensed list. *Harness:* names, ₹ amounts and visa words heard exactly. |
| 2 | **Before departure** | Doesn't know the employer's name, phone or address, or the embassy number. | A checklist: employer contact, work visa (not visit), embassy helpline | No one asks these questions. | **Departure check (voice, Telugu):** "Do you have the employer's name and number? Is it a work visa?" Missing answers mean a risk flag. |
| 3 | **Airport arrival** | Nobody comes. A **stranger** takes them, and he is not their real sponsor. | Pickup verified against the visa/employer name, plus someone to call | Language barrier, no phone, no one knows they've landed. | **Arrival check-in (first 24–48 h):** "Who picked you up? What's his name? Where are you going?" This is compared with the promise record, and a mismatch alerts the family. *Harness:* Arabic names and places heard right. |
| 4 | **Passport taken** | Passport is held by the "arbab". | Passport always with the worker. This is illegal to confiscate in Saudi (fine reported as SAR 5,000, labour law amended in 2019) and the UAE | The worker doesn't know it's illegal. Enforcement is weak. | Flag on "passport teeskunnaru" / "passport kafeel daggara undi" (passport taken / it's with the kafeel). The worker is told in Telugu that this is illegal. |
| 5 | **Wrong job** | Herding goats in the desert instead of the promised job. | The job matches the contract. | Nobody compares what was promised with what is happening. | **Check-in compares job, salary and hours** against the stored promise. A mismatch raises a risk. |
| 6 | **Abuse** | No pay, beatings, little water and food. | Wages paid (UAE uses a wage protection system, WPS **[verify scope]**), a complaint channel | Can't call, can't explain, afraid. | **Distress and meaning guard:** "kodutunnaru" (they're beating me), "jeetham ivvaledu" (salary not paid), "neellu/annam ivvatledu" (no water/food). Negation must be heard right: *ichhadu* (paid) vs *ivvaledu* (not paid). |
| 7 | **Isolation for years** | Family and government don't know for a very long time. | Regular contact | Help is **reactive**: the worker must reach out, but he can't. | **Silence is a signal.** Missed check-ins escalate: family, then a helpline or volunteer, then a case file. Gulf Saathi can do this without a perfect transcript. |
| 8 | **Language barrier** | Obeys orders he doesn't understand and can't explain himself to anyone. | An interpreter | Telugu worker, Arabic employer, English government forms. | **Safe translation** with locked terms, plus a glossary of key Arabic words explained in Telugu (kafeel, iqama, huroob, final exit). |
| 9 | **Escape → legal trap** | Runs away through the desert, ends up in a police and deportation process. Running away makes him the "illegal" one. | A legal path: embassy, labour complaint, job transfer | Absconding (**huroob**) status. HRW says workers with absconding charges don't benefit from Saudi's 2021 job-mobility reforms. | Explains *huroob* in Telugu **before** he runs, and builds a **ready case file** (promise vs reality, dates, evidence) for the embassy or MADAD instead. |
| 10 | **Return** | Years lost, no wages, no compensation. | Recovery of dues | No evidence. | The recorded promise and the check-ins **become the evidence**. |

## 2. The major problems (root causes, not symptoms)

1. **No record of the promise.** Everything is verbal on phone calls, so later complaints fail for lack of evidence.
2. **Information gap.** The agent knows the rules and the worker doesn't: the fee cap, work visa vs visit visa, the passport law, what huroob means.
3. **Help is reactive.** The embassy, MADAD and police need the worker to find them, explain in their language and prove it. A man in the desert can't do any of that.
4. **Language, three ways.** Telugu (worker), Arabic (employer), English (government). The worker is "blind" at every step, as in your Mars example.
5. **Nobody owns the whole journey.** The village agent, the state, the central government (MEA, Protector of Emigrants), the foreign government and the employer each see only one piece.
6. **Fear and debt.** The loan taken for the agent fee keeps people silent and stuck, and running away makes them "illegal".
7. **Low literacy.** Voice is the only channel many workers can use. **That is why voice AI has to hear correctly.** For them there is no other way in.
8. **Telangana-specific: the visit-visa trap.** Reporting describes a Jagtial agency promising employment visas but sending people on visit visas, and agents telling workers a tourist visa "will become an employment visa in a few months."

## 4. Product insights from this (what changes in our build)

1. **The first 48 hours after landing are the most dangerous** (stage 3). A check-in on arrival is the single highest-value call.
2. **Silence is data.** Missing check-ins must escalate, even when no transcript exists.
3. **Evidence before damage.** The recorded promise call *is* the future case file.
4. **Ask few, simple questions in Telugu that a scared person can answer by voice.**
5. **Coded distress (idea, needs care).** Sometimes the worker can't say "help" because the employer is listening. A pre-agreed safe phrase that the harness must catch exactly is a real harness use-case. It needs ethical care: false alarms, and the danger of discovery.
6. **Negation and distress words are the highest-stakes errors.** *ichhadu* vs *ivvaledu* decides whether a "salary not paid" risk fires at all.

## 5. Demo scenario idea (fictional, Telangana)

A fictional worker from a Jagtial mandal, told in **six calls across his journey**. Each call is a benchmark clip:

1. **Promise call:** agent promises a driver job, 1,500 AED, ₹1,50,000 fee, "work visa".
2. **Departure call:** "visa visit visa ani raasi undi" (the visa says visit visa). This triggers the **visit-visa flag**.
3. **Arrival call:** a different person picked him up, the name doesn't match, and "passport teeskunnaru".
4. **Week 2:** "jeetham ivvaledu, edari lo mekalu kaayistunnanu" (salary not paid, I'm herding goats in the desert). Salary and job mismatch.
5. **Missed check-ins:** a silence escalation.
6. **Distress call** (bad line, noise): "kodutunnaru, kapadandi" (they're beating me, save me). Escalate to a human, and generate the case file.

Show the **base model vs Saathi** on each. One line for the pitch: *"Goat Life happened because nobody heard him. Saathi hears, before it's too late."*
